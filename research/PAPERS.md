# Annotated paper library

Collected 22 September 2026: **46 sources; 31 PDFs downloaded locally; 15 references linked without a local PDF.**

Use the [research guide](README.md) for reading order and the [repo audit](REPO_AUDIT.md) for implications. “Established” may describe a theoretical framework; it does not mean every effect calculated within it has been detected. Assessments below are this collection’s appraisal. Most entries received metadata/abstract review plus selected sections, not a full independent derivation.

Local PDF links work in this workspace. PDFs are excluded from git; fresh clones can retrieve the exact URLs recorded in [sources.json](sources.json). Citation keys match [references.bib](references.bib).

## Foundations

### R01 — Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in the Quantum Theory

**Aharonov, Y. and Bohm, D. (1959).** Physical Review 115, 485–491.

**Assessment:** Established quantum effect.

Read first to separate gauge-invariant interference from a proposed new local force. The Aharonov–Bohm phase does not, by itself, establish a locally measurable divergence of A or anomalous gravity.

[Source](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.115.485) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.115.485) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R02 — Further Discussion of the Role of Electromagnetic Potentials in the Quantum Theory

**Aharonov, Y. and Bohm, D. (1963).** Physical Review 130, 1625–1632.

**Assessment:** Historical theoretical extension.

Trace the AB electrodynamics lineage here. Distinguish this extension from the experimentally established 1959 interference effect; experimental confirmation of that effect does not validate every later modification.

[Source](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.130.1625) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.130.1625) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R03 — Uniqueness theorems for classical four-vector fields in Euclidean and Minkowski spaces

**Woodside, Dale A. (1999).** Journal of Mathematical Physics 40, 4911–4943.

**Assessment:** Mathematical result.

The source behind the repo’s uniqueness claims. A reconstruction or uniqueness theorem needs its hypotheses and boundary data; it does not establish which dynamical action nature uses or experimentally demonstrate a new polarization.

[Source](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/uniqueness-theorems-for-classical-four-vector-fields-in-euclidean/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.533007) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R04 — Three-vector and scalar field identities and uniqueness theorems in Euclidean and Minkowski spaces

**Woodside, Dale A. (2009).** American Journal of Physics 77, 438–446.

**Assessment:** Mathematical result.

A shorter route into the decomposition theorems and their transverse/longitudinal classes. Keep field decomposition distinct from a Hamiltonian count of physical degrees of freedom.

[Source](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/three-vector-and-scalar-field-identities-and-uniqueness-theorems-/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.3076300) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R05 — The Stueckelberg Field

**Ruegg, Henri and Ruiz-Altaba, Marti (2004).** International Journal of Modern Physics A 19, 3265–3347.

**Assessment:** Established formalism; model-dependent applications.

Explains the gauge-invariant massive-vector construction. Useful for preventing the conflation of a Stueckelberg compensator, a gauge-fixing term, and a new observable scalar. These are different physical commitments.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0304245) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217751X04019755) · [Local PDF · 58 pages](papers/R05.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:hep-th/0304245v2; first submitted 2003/04/29. The journal version may differ.

Crossref lists pages 3265–3347; arXiv journal-reference text lists 3265–3348. Publisher-registered pagination is used here.

## EED and gravity proposals

### R14 — On the electromagnetic nature of dark energy and the origin of cosmic magnetic fields

**Beltran Jimenez, Jose and Maroto, Antonio L. (2011).** arXiv preprint / conference contribution.

**Assessment:** Speculative cosmological model.

A physical scalar state is proposed in expanding spacetime. The authors distinguish gravitational excitation from excitation by conserved charged currents. A cosmological homogeneous mode is not evidence that a tabletop coil generates the same mode.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.2072) · [Local PDF · 9 pages](papers/R14.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:1101.2072v1; first submitted 2011/01/11. The journal version may differ.

### R15 — A new theory of tensor-scalar gravity coupled to Aharonov-Bohm electrodynamics

**Minotti, F. and Modanese, G. (2025).** Modern Physics Letters A 40, 2550023.

**Assessment:** Speculative action-based model.

Closest direct match to this project. Couples AB electrodynamics to gravitational scalars and studies a weak-field limit. Unknown couplings and an anomalous source sector prevent a parameter-free gravity prediction. Re-derive the complete varied action before implementing it.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05230) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732325500233) · [Local PDF · 23 pages](papers/R15.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:2408.05230v3; first submitted 2024/08/01. The journal version may differ.

## Established EM–gravity coupling

### R20 — Geons

**Wheeler, John Archibald (1955).** Physical Review 97, 511–536.

**Assessment:** Theoretical solutions within general relativity.

A concrete historical study of electromagnetic energy gravitating strongly enough to help confine itself. Useful for understanding genuine nonlinear EM–gravity interaction; it does not provide evidence for controllable laboratory antigravity.

[Source](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.97.511) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.97.511) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R21 — Wave Resonance of Light and Gravitational Waves

**Gertsenshtein, M. E. (1962).** Soviet Physics JETP 14, 84–85.

**Assessment:** Prediction of Einstein–Maxwell theory.

Foundational calculation of conversion between electromagnetic and gravitational waves in an external field. English translation 1962; Russian original 1961. This is a real theoretical coupling mechanism, not a claim of laboratory detection or useful conversion efficiency.

[Source](https://jetp.ras.ru/cgi-bin/e/index/e/14/1/p84?a=list) · [Local PDF · 2 pages](papers/R21.pdf).

### R22 — Gravitoelectromagnetism: A Brief Review

**Mashhoon, Bahram (2007).** The Measurement of Gravitomagnetism: A Challenging Enterprise, pp. 29–39.

**Assessment:** Weak-field GR formalism.

Read before interpreting the GEM module. Gravitoelectric and gravitomagnetic fields describe weak gravity sourced by stress-energy and mass currents; similar-looking equations do not make these fields identical to electromagnetic E and B. Preprint 2003, chapter 2007, revision 2008.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0311030) · [Local PDF · 15 pages](papers/R22.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:gr-qc/0311030v2; first submitted 2003/11/08. The journal version may differ.

### R23 — Potential of radio telescopes as high-frequency gravitational wave detectors

**Domcke, Valerie and Garcia-Cely, Camilo (2021).** Physical Review Letters 126, 021104.

**Assessment:** Standard-theory conversion; observational proposal/limits.

Modern treatment of photon–graviton conversion and radio observations. Supplies realistic scale and propagation considerations. The bounds depend on assumptions about cosmic magnetic fields; they are not a detection of conversion.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01161) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.126.021104) · [Local PDF · 12 pages](papers/R23.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:2006.01161v3; first submitted 2020/06/01. The journal version may differ.

### R24 — Gravitational Wave Scattering on Magnetic Fields

**Domcke, Valerie and Garcia-Cely, Camilo and Lee, Sung Mook (2025).** arXiv preprint.

**Assessment:** Recent calculation within standard coupling.

Develops angular and polarization structure for inverse Gertsenshtein scattering, including nonuniform magnetic fields. A strong target for eventual geometry-based simulation. Publication status here follows the retrieved arXiv record, which lists no journal reference.

## Geometric and quantum connections

### R25 — Kaluza-Klein Gravity

**Overduin, J. M. and Wesson, P. S. (1997).** Physics Reports 283, 303–380.

**Assessment:** Unconfirmed higher-dimensional unification framework.

Explains how a higher-dimensional metric decomposes into a four-dimensional metric, vector, and scalar. It does not imply that the four-dimensional Newtonian potential equals a constant times the electromagnetic four-divergence. Journal 1997; arXiv deposit 1998.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9805018) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0370-1573(96)00046-4) · [Local PDF · 102 pages](papers/R25.pdf).

### R26 — Black holes and the double copy

**Monteiro, Ricardo and O'Connell, Donal and White, Chris D. (2014).** Journal of High Energy Physics 2014, 056.

**Assessment:** Mathematical relation between theories.

A concrete gauge/gravity correspondence for classes of classical solutions. Excellent for analytic cross-checks. Mapping a Coulomb-like solution to a black-hole metric is not a physical device converting a given electromagnetic source into that black hole.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0239) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP12(2014)056) · [Local PDF · 22 pages](papers/R26.pdf).

## Other extensions and effective theories

### R29 — Foundations of the New Field Theory

**Born, Max and Infeld, Leopold (1934).** Proceedings of the Royal Society A 144, 425–451.

**Assessment:** Nonlinear electrodynamics model.

A historically important nonlinear extension addressing point-charge field behavior. Distinguish nonlinear functions of electromagnetic invariants from the AB scalar-divergence extension. Coupling Born–Infeld theory to gravity is an additional, explicit construction.

[Source](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2935568) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1934.0059) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R30 — Consequences of Dirac Theory of the Positron

**Heisenberg, W. and Euler, H. (1936).** Zeitschrift fuer Physik 98, 714–732.

**Assessment:** Established QED effective-theory foundation.

Shows why classical Maxwell equations acquire nonlinear quantum corrections. The linked file is the 2006 English translation of the 1936 paper. Provides a standard-physics comparator for claims that nonlinear electromagnetic behavior necessarily indicates a new gravitational interaction.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0605038) · [Local PDF · 27 pages](papers/R30.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:physics/0605038v1; first submitted 2006/05/04. The journal version may differ.

### R31 — QED vacuum polarization in a background gravitational field and its effect on the velocity of photons

**Drummond, I. T. and Hathrell, S. J. (1980).** Physical Review D 22, 343.

**Assessment:** QED in curved spacetime; effective approximation.

Derives curvature-dependent electromagnetic terms from QED. Useful for studying actual operators coupling curvature and electromagnetism. Low-energy propagation speeds must not be identified automatically with high-frequency signal/front velocities or an operational causality violation.

[Source](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.22.343) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.22.343) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

## Experimental constraints and controls

### R32 — The Confrontation between General Relativity and Experiment

**Will, Clifford M. (2014).** Living Reviews in Relativity 17, 4.

**Assessment:** Review of tested gravitational physics.

Reference for equivalence-principle and post-Newtonian requirements on any proposed coupling. Use as a framework, not as a table of the latest limits: the numerical experimental situation has advanced since 2014.

### R33 — MICROSCOPE mission: final results of the test of the Equivalence Principle

**Touboul, Pierre and others (2022).** Physical Review Letters 129, 121102.

**Assessment:** Precision null experiment.

Constrains composition-dependent free fall of titanium and platinum test bodies. A proposed electromagnetic contribution to gravity must be checked against the appropriate predicted differential acceleration. This is not a universal upper limit on every possible EM–gravity coupling.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15487) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.129.121102) · [Local PDF · 9 pages](papers/R33.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:2209.15487v1; first submitted 2022/09/30. The journal version may differ.

### R34 — A test of electric charge conservation with Borexino

**Agostini, M. and others (2015).** Physical Review Letters 115, 231802.

**Assessment:** Channel-specific null experiment.

Bounds electron decay into a neutrino and photon: lifetime greater than 6.6 × 10^28 years at 90% confidence. It constrains that channel, not automatically every proposed nonlocal transport anomaly. A material-specific nonconservation claim needs its own quantitative test.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01223) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.115.231802) · [Local PDF · 5 pages](papers/R34.pdf).

Reading copy: arXiv:1509.01223v2; first submitted 2015/09/03. The journal version may differ.

### R35 — Photon and Graviton Mass Limits

**Goldhaber, Alfred Scharff and Nieto, Michael Martin (2010).** Reviews of Modern Physics 82, 939–979.

**Assessment:** Review of model-dependent constraints.

Useful for Proca and extra-polarization branches, especially distinctions between robust and assumption-dependent bounds. This is a historical review, not a claim that its numerical limits remain the strongest in 2026.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1003) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.82.939) · [Local PDF · 42 pages](papers/R35.pdf).

**Assessment:** Conventional explanation of tested devices.

Control literature for gas-mediated capacitor thrust: the measured forces were consistent with ion transport and collisions transferring momentum to air. This is not a direct test of the user’s specified static solid-dielectric claim. For that question prioritize R38, R42, and R43.

### R37 — A method of and an apparatus or machine for producing force or motion

**Brown, Thomas Townsend (1928).** British patent GB300311A.

**Assessment:** Original historical claim; patent, not validation.

The patent describes rigidly linked conductors and alternating insulating slabs and plates. Useful for defining Brown’s solid-dielectric claim in his own terms. Its assertion of gravitational force is a claim to test, not an experimental finding established by patent publication.

### R38 — Twenty First Century Propulsion Concept

**Talley, Robert L. (1991).** Phillips Laboratory report PL-TR-91-3009; DTIC ADA237853.

**Assessment:** Experimental null for steady operation; separate breakdown anomalies.

Public-release report with high-vacuum capacitor tests, limited to about 19 kV by breakdown. Reports no detected propulsion under steady, non-breakdown conditions; limited ceramic breakdown observations are a separate transient claim. Chamber-wall electrostatic interactions were significant.

[Source](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA237853.pdf) · [Local PDF · 102 pages](papers/R38.pdf).

### R39 — Exploratory Research on the Phenomenon of the Movement of High Voltage Capacitors

**Buehler, Doyle R. (2004).** Journal of Space Mixing 2, 1–22.

**Assessment:** Positive exploratory report; mechanism unresolved.

Reports apparent upward forces in charged capacitor experiments. Includes air and wax dielectrics, and extrapolates possible vacuum operation rather than demonstrating it. Preserve the positive claim, but do not label it an established vacuum solid-dielectric effect or infer gravity from a balance reading.

[Source](https://www.space-mixing-theory.com/abstract2.htm) · [Local PDF · 22 pages](papers/R39.pdf).

### R40 — On the gravitational field induced by static electromagnetic sources

**Ivanov, Boyko V. (2005).** arXiv preprint.

**Assessment:** Proposed laboratory interpretation of exact GR solutions.

Argues for strong acceleration terms in Weyl–Majumdar–Papapetrou solutions sourced by static electromagnetic configurations. Audit material mass, surface sources, boundary matching and supports before interpreting a term linear in field as laboratory propulsion. Compare the specific predictions with R42–R43.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0502047) · [Local PDF · 18 pages](papers/R40.pdf).

### R41 — Testing the Possibility of Weight Changes in Highly-Polarized Electrets

**Schreiber, T. and Tajmar, M. (2016).** 52nd AIAA/SAE/ASEE Joint Propulsion Conference, AIAA 2016-4919.

**Assessment:** Related polarization experiment; no confirmed weight effect.

Polarized and control wax electrets showed similar weight drift; improved sealing reduced artifacts. This is a useful static-polarization control, but its carnauba/rosin/beeswax mixture differs from the trichlorobenzene-containing mixture behind the tested claim. It is not an exact replication of every electret or Brown device.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2016-4919) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2016-4919) · [Local PDF · 11 pages](papers/R41.pdf).

### R42 — Put strong limits on all proposed theories so far assessing electrostatic propulsion: Does a charged high-voltage capacitor produce thrust?

**Tajmar, M. and Schreiber, T. (2020).** Journal of Electrostatics 107, 103477.

**Assessment:** Direct solid-dielectric capacitor test; null within sensitivity.

Explicitly investigates capacitor force claims without ionization, using ceramic/PTFE and varied electrode/dielectric geometry. No detected force up to 10 kV at ±0.3 mg accuracy (about 2.9 micronewtons). A directly relevant experimental constraint, with material, voltage and sensitivity limits; not a universal no-go theorem.

[Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304388620300619) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.elstat.2020.103477) · [Local PDF · 23 pages](papers/R42.pdf).

### R43 — In-depth experimental search for a coupling between gravity and electromagnetism with steady fields

**Tajmar, Martin and Kößling, Matthias and Neunzig, Oliver (2024).** Scientific Reports 14, 19427.

**Assessment:** Direct steady-field tests; configuration-specific null results.

High-vacuum tests include low/high-permittivity solid capacitors, asymmetric and gradient dielectrics, and magnetic configurations. Authors report no anomalous capacitor force; sensitivity varies by configuration. This directly addresses the static branch. Use the per-device results and controls rather than treating a headline nanonewton sensitivity as universal.

### R44 — Enhancing electrostatic charge stability of corona charged Teflon electret films for radiation dosimetry by optimizing metal electrode backing material

**Rabiee, Sahel and Sohrabi, Mehdi and Afarideh, Hossein (2024).** Applied Radiation and Isotopes 205, 111187.

**Assessment:** PTFE retained-charge mechanism; different apparatus.

Demonstrates that metal electrode backing influences charge retention in deliberately corona-charged PTFE electret films. Supports treating retained charge as a material variable; does not demonstrate electret buildup or force suppression in the R42/R43 capacitor experiments.

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38245996/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apradiso.2024.111187) · No local PDF: reference link retained.

### R45 — Kinetics of ferroelectric switching in poled barium titanate ceramics: Effects of electrical cycling rate

**Kannan, Vignesh and Trassin, Morgan and Kochmann, Dennis M. (2022).** Materialia 25, 101553.

**Assessment:** Direct BaTiO3 polarization and strain experiment.

Measures rate-dependent polarization switching and strain in poled BaTiO3 ceramics. A relevant constitutive-model source for the next asymmetric-capacitor benchmark: remanence, coercivity and deformation cannot be captured by a single constant permittivity.

### R46 — High-field conduction in barium titanate

**Morrison, F. D. and Zubko, P. and Jung, D. J. and Scott, J. F. and Baxter, P. and Saad, M. M. and Bowman, R. M. and Gregg, J. M. (2005).** Applied Physics Letters 86, 152903.

**Assessment:** BaTiO3 transport mechanism; scale-dependent applicability.

Reports high-field space-charge-limited conduction associated with traps in thin BaTiO3 crystals, with comparison to multilayer ceramics. Establishes a transport mechanism to check; it does not determine the leakage of a chosen bulk dielectric or supply an anomalous force law.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0409585) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1886899) · [Local PDF · 15 pages](papers/R46.pdf).

Journal author order follows the Queen’s University Belfast publication record. The preprint title page places Gregg before Bowman, and arXiv catalog metadata starts with Scott; these records differ in ordering.
